Notes compiled 2026-10-08. Labels used throughout:
- **[VERIFIED-PLATFORM]**: figure read off a live platform page or scraped dataset (member counts, list prices). It shows a snapshot, not revenue.
- **[SELF-REPORTED]**: creator, agency or company claim with no independent audit.
- **[VENDOR-BLOG]**: SEO or tool-vendor blog. Treat as directional only.
- **[NEXLEV]**: NexLev analytics or promotions database (scraped Aug–Sep 2026). AdSense figures are modeled estimates, and product prices come from scraped video descriptions and landing pages.

## Q1. What digital products do AI persona / AI video creators sell, at what prices, on which platforms? (named examples)

### Takeaway
The dominant 2026 model among AI-video educator channels is: free lead magnet or free Skool community → paid Skool/Whop membership ($3–$184/mo) → done-for-you service or agency upsell. Affiliate links to AI tools (Higgsfield, OpenArt, HeyGen) sit in every description. Low-ticket "AI influencer" assets such as prompt packs, LoRAs and character packs sell for $2–$50 on Etsy and Gumroad, but sales data is thin and pricing is a race to the bottom. The biggest verified recurring-revenue signals come from AI *automation* educators (n8n/Make/Claude) on Skool, not from AI-influencer educators.

### Inferences
- At list price, Nate Herk's paid tier (3,700 × $129) implies about **$477K/month gross**, and Nick Saraev's Maker School (2,400 × $184) about **$442K/month gross**. These are upper-bound inferences: they ignore discounts, annual billing, churn, refunds and fees, and member counts may include lapsed or comped members. Even so, they dwarf AdSense for these channels.
- AI-*influencer* education specifically (Lotti, Isa, the Whop AI-influencer academies) shows much smaller verified traction: hundreds of members, not thousands. The money in "AI persona" education in 2026 sits mostly in (a) AI-UGC-for-brands and (b) automation/agency education. Selling "make your own AI influencer" courses is crowded and has low conversion.
- The most common stack among AI-video educators is: Kit/Skool free lead magnet → $30–$190/mo Skool/Whop community → consultation or agency service. Gumroad/Etsy one-off assets ($5–$50) are a side income.
- n8n/Make automation templates and Notion databases appear mainly *inside* paid Skool communities as "resource libraries". I found no standalone storefront with verified sales figures for them.

## Q2. What are the funnels (lead magnet → list/community → paid; link-in-bio; comment-to-DM; YouTube Products shelf; TikTok Shop)?

### Takeaway
Funnels in 2026 are built around **per-video tracked links to a free community or lead magnet**: the video description or pinned comment links to a free Skool or Kit opt-in, which upsells to a paid tier. On short-form, **link-in-bio stores (Stan) and comment-keyword-to-DM automation (ManyChat, Stan AutoDM)** carry the traffic. YouTube's Products section is used for self-promotion. YouTube Shopping affiliate and TikTok Shop are not available to Pakistan-resident creators.

### Cited Findings
- **Per-video UTM tracking into a free Skool group.** Nate Herk's description links carry `el=<video-slug>&hcategory=youtube-videos&utm_campaign=free-group` (for example `el=claude-delete`, `el=codex-browser-use`). The free group (about 440K members) then upsells to a $129/mo "Plus" tier [NEXLEV] — [Skool link example](https://www.skool.com/ai-automation-society/about?el=claude-delete&hcategory=youtube-videos&utm_campaign=free-group)
- **Pinned comment to academy.** AI With Lotti "directs viewers to the pinned comment" for her AI LUXE Academy and AI CRE8R community (videos from July 2026) [NEXLEV] — [Skool: AI CRE8R](https://skool.com/aiwithlotti/about)
- **Email lead magnets.** Isa does AI uses free Kit-hosted ebooks ("AI Influencer Playbook", "AI Avatar Money Kit") as list builders next to affiliate links [NEXLEV] — [Kit page](https://isa-does-ai.kit.com/dc7a499448)
- **YouTube Products section.** Nick Saraev's free community is "listed in the Products section" of his videos [NEXLEV] — [nicksaraev.com](https://nicksaraev.com)
- **Free Whop as a lead magnet.** "Whop AI UGC" is a free group with 226 members [VERIFIED-PLATFORM] — [Whop](https://whop.com/discover/whop-creators-ugc/whop-ai-ugc/)
- **ManyChat comment-to-DM.** ManyChat restructured to five tiers in March 2026 and cut the free tier from 1,000 to 25 contacts. Plans run from $0 to $139/mo, and contacts accumulate without a monthly reset, so a viral post can force an upgrade. Active creators typically pay $29–$69/mo including overages, plus about $29/mo for the AI add-on. Meta's API allows it only when the user triggers it by commenting or DMing first [VENDOR-BLOG] — [Featurebase: ManyChat review](https://www.featurebase.app/blog/manychat-review); [Jupiter: ManyChat for creators](https://www.jupiter.co/blog/manychat-for-food-bloggers-worth-it-2026); [RoboRhythms](https://www.roborhythms.com/manychat-review/)
- **Stan Store** reportedly includes "AutoDM" (comment-to-DM) in the Creator plan. Only one source confirms this [VENDOR-BLOG] — [Schoolmaker: Stan pricing](https://schoolmaker.com/blog/stan-store-pricing)
- **YouTube Shopping affiliate program.** As of 2026-10-01 it covers 14 markets: Argentina, Brazil, India, Indonesia, Japan, Korea, Malaysia, Mexico, the Philippines, Singapore, Taiwan, Thailand, the US and Vietnam. **Pakistan is not included.** YouTube plans to reach 35 countries by end-2026 (list unnamed). Eligibility requires residing in a supported country [NEWS/TRADE] — [Sirency](https://www.sirency.com/blog/youtube-shopping-affiliate-program); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-creators-in-35-markets-to-earn-shopping-commissions-by-year-end/)
- **TikTok Shop and AI content.** One guide says Shop commissions are not explicitly banned for AI content, while Creator Rewards excludes it (see Q4) [VENDOR-BLOG] — [Shortformnation](https://www.shortformnation.com/blog/ai-generated-ugc-for-tiktok-shop-what-actually-works-and-what-gets-you-demonetized-2026-guide)
- **Fanvue funnel for AI personas.** The typical operator stack is Instagram/TikTok/Threads persona content → link-in-bio → Fanvue subscription plus paid DMs, with brand deals layered on top. The top accounts run four or more revenue streams [VENDOR-BLOG] — [InstantDM](https://instantdm.com/blog/how-ai-influencers-make-money-2026); [AI Journal case study](https://aijourn.com/a-brave-new-world-of-ai-influencers/)

### Inferences
- For a faceless AI-avatar YouTube channel, the highest-leverage funnel is: one free resource per video (prompt pack, character sheet, workflow) behind an email or free-Skool gate with a per-video tracking tag, then a single paid tier. That matches what the scraped top channels do.
- Comment-keyword DM funnels are cheap on Instagram and Facebook but get costly with virality on ManyChat's contact-based billing. Stan's flat fee is more predictable, but Stan depends on Stripe or PayPal, which matters in Pakistan (see Q8).

### Gaps
- I have no conversion-rate data from verified sources (free → paid, comment → DM → sale).
- I could not confirm Stan AutoDM inclusion or TikTok Shop availability per country from official pages.

## Q3. How do AI virtual influencers earn from brand deals in 2025–2026 (rates, agencies, examples)?

### Takeaway
Brand-deal income for AI personas is real but concentrated in a handful of agency-run characters. Nearly all figures are agency-reported and date from 2023–2024. Per-post rates quoted for virtual influencers ($1K–$15K) are vendor-blog numbers. Independent operators should expect $100–$400 test deals in months 4–6. The economics favor AI personas on **margin**, not on rate.

- By 2026 coverage, Aitana had nearly **400K followers** combined and an **11-person team**. ### Inferences
- For a new AI persona, brand money arrives first from **AI tool companies** (affiliate links, Higgsfield Earn-style per-view bounties, small sponsorships). Consumer brands come later and pay roughly the same CPM-equivalent as for human creators.
- The agency model (The Clueless) is a margin play: one 11-person team runs characters that cost little per post. Solo operators cannot copy the rate card, but they can copy the margin.
- Engagement for virtual influencers can be far lower than for humans (0.6% versus 3.6% in the Miquela/BMW example), which caps rates as brands become more performance-focused.

### Gaps
- I found no audited 2025–2026 per-post fee for any named virtual influencer. Most figures are 2023–2024 and agency-sourced.

## Q4. How much does a faceless or AI channel earn from ads (YouTube RPM by niche, size and geography; TikTok Creator Rewards; Instagram/Facebook payouts)?

AdSense is usually the *smallest* line for AI-video channels. ## Q7. Licensing AI characters/IP, merch and kids' book spin-offs

The clearest proof that AI-born characters can become licensed IP is **"Italian Brainrot"**. ## Q8. Practical: what does a Pakistan-based creator need to know (YouTube/AdSense, payments, Stripe workarounds, local-market products)?

### Takeaway
YouTube ad revenue works in Pakistan under the *standard* YPP thresholds. The expanded 500-subscriber tier reportedly excludes Pakistan. The hard part is selling digital products, because **Stripe and PayPal are unavailable** and Stripe-based platforms (Skool, Stan, Gumroad, likely Lemon Squeezy) cannot pay out to Pakistan. **Whop reportedly began PKR bank payouts to Pakistan around March 2026**, the most important development for this creator. Payoneer remains the default receiving account. Wise is restricted. US/UK LLC workarounds are legal only if done properly, and borrowing a relative's foreign account violates platform terms.

### Cited Findings

**YouTube, Facebook and TikTok in Pakistan**
- Pakistani creators can earn from Shorts under YouTube's Shorts revenue-sharing (ProPakistani, Jan 2023; title-level evidence) — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2023/01/12/pakistani-youtubers-can-now-earn-more-money-using-shorts/amp/)

## Q9. Ethics and legal: FTC disclosure for AI influencers and affiliates, refunds, course-selling backlash, misleading income claims

### Takeaway
US law already covers AI personas. The FTC's 2023 Endorsement Guides treat virtual or AI endorsers as endorsers, bar them from claiming personal experience with products, and require material-connection disclosure for affiliate links and sponsorships. The bigger 2025–2026 risk for "make money with AI" course sellers is **earnings-claim enforcement**. The FTC banned the Growth Cave operators (who sold "YouTube automation" via YouTube ads), Click Profit and Air AI from selling business opportunities. Platform refund disputes on Whop and Skool are common.

### Cited Findings
- **FTC Endorsement Guides (June 2023)**: the definition of "endorser" covers virtual and AI-created figures. A virtual influencer **cannot claim personal experience** ("I love this coffee"). Avatar creators are liable for statements they know or should know are false. There is no explicit rule that an avatar must say it is not human, *unless* omitting that would mislead. Material-connection disclosure applies equally to avatars — [Dynamis LLP](https://dynamisllp.com/knowledge/ai-disclosure-in-2026-recent-developments-and-practical-steps-for-brands-and-influencers); [HireInfluence](https://hireinfluence.com/blog/influencer-marketing-ftc-guidelines/)
- A July 2026 vendor blog claims an FTC "double disclosure" rule (sponsorship *and* AI use). **Not corroborated by any FTC primary source** — [Beancount blog](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/13/ftc-ai-content-double-disclosure-rule-influencers-guide)
- **South Korea's FTC** amended its endorsement guidelines effective **June 1, 2026** with concrete AI virtual-influencer disclosure requirements — [Cheongchul](https://cheongchul.com/en/blog/ai-가상인물-광고-표시방법-3가지-체크리스트-부당광고)
- **Platform AI labels**: TikTok requires labels on realistic AI scenes or people — [Dynamis LLP](https://dynamisllp.com/knowledge/ai-disclosure-in-2026-recent-developments-and-practical-steps-for-brands-and-influencers). Etsy and KDP require AI disclosure (see Q7)
- **FTC v. Growth Cave**: the complaint (Feb 2025) alleges about **$50M taken via false income claims**. Its "Knowledge Business Accelerator" was sold via YouTube ads, lifestyle videos were used as "proof", testimonials were reused, and $6,800 was charged upfront. In the **January 2026 settlement** the co-CEOs are permanently banned from selling business opportunities and must liquidate assets. One defendant's order carries a **$48.6M judgment**, partially suspended — [FTC press release (Mar 2025)](https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/03/ftc-takes-action-stop-sprawling-growth-cave-business-opportunity-credit-repair-scam); [FTC settlement (Jan 2026)](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/01/ftc-secures-settlement-banning-growth-cave-defendants-marketing-selling-business-opportunities); [Complaint PDF](https://ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/GrowthCave-Complaint.pdf)
- **FTC "Operation AI Comply"** (from Sept 2024):
  - **Click Profit** promised "passive income" via an AI-powered e-commerce system. A fifth of customers earned nothing, and another third under $2,500. It was banned in August 2025 with judgments over $20M — [Benesch Law](https://www.beneschlaw.com/resources/one-year-in-ftcs-operation-ai-comply-continues-under-new-administration-signaling-enduring-enforcement-focus.html)
  - **Air AI** made false earnings claims ("earn back tens of thousands within 30 days") and refused refunds despite its own guarantee, taking about $19M. It was banned on **March 24, 2026**, with an **$18M judgment, largely suspended ($50K paid)** — [CFO Dive](https://cfodive.com/news/ai-startup-settles-ftc-deception-charges/815892/); [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/330074)
- **Refund and chargeback complaints (self-reported, unverified)**:
  - Whop denied a refund because the creator's no-refund policy was accepted at checkout — [Trustpilot: Whop](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/whop.com?page=9)
  - A Skool "AI With Gary" buyer paid $150 plus $47/mo for 8 months, says they were removed on a billing day and refused a refund, and filed a bank dispute — [Trustpilot: AiWithGary](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/aiwithgary.com)
  - AI Pro Academy: a $49.99 offer reportedly became a $246.99 charge — [Trustpilot: AI Pro Academy](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/ai-pro.academy)
- **Fanvue chargeback fee**: $50 per dispute above a 1.5% chargeback rate — [OFM Legal](https://ofmlegal.com/en/blog/ofm/what-is-fanvue/)
- **Income-claim skepticism in the AI-influencer niche**: agency figures for Aitana are "a best month, not an average month". Many published AI-influencer income "examples" are composites (for example RYLA's headline example is labeled composite) — [Avatar Factory](https://avatarfactory.io/ai-influencers/aitana-lopez); [RYLA](https://ryla.ai/en/blog/ai-influencer-income/)
- **YouTube risk for AI personas as fake experts**: a single, unverified source says AI personas presenting as human experts on health, legal, finance or politics are named as non-monetizable — [NoobClaw](https://noobclaw.com/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-2026/)

### Inferences
- Safe-practice checklist for an AI-avatar creator selling products:
  1. Label the persona as AI in the bio and on videos, using platform AI labels.
  2. Disclose affiliate links and sponsorships clearly ("#ad", "affiliate link") near the link, not hidden.
  3. Never have the avatar claim first-person product experience.
  4. Make no specific income promises in course marketing. Use typical-results disclaimers and show distribution, not best cases.
  5. Publish and honor a clear refund policy, since non-honored guarantees were a core count against Air AI.
  6. Avoid "done-for-you passive income" framing, which is exactly what the FTC's business-opportunity cases targeted.
- Selling to US buyers from Pakistan still exposes the seller to FTC jurisdiction, and to platform bans and chargebacks, which are the more immediate enforcement mechanism.

### Gaps
- I could not verify any 2026 FTC rule specific to AI-content disclosure from ftc.gov.
- I found no Reddit or press coverage of specific 2026 backlash against named AI-influencer course sellers. Only Trustpilot complaints, which are unverified, plus the 2024 Li Yizhou case.
- Pakistani consumer-protection and e-commerce law (and PECA) as it applies to digital-product sales and AI personas was not researched.
